⛓How it works

The model reads.
Arithmetic decides.

AI is good at reading messy text from forty different banks. It is not something you should trust with your money. So Alertproof splits the job in three.

1 · Read

Gemma reads the alert

Every bank words its alerts differently and changes them often. Gemma, an open-weight model, turns any alert into the same fields: credit or debit, amount, balance, account digits, reference, time.

2 · Ground

Code checks what Gemma read

Every number must be written in the alert itself. So must the bank name and the credit/debit word. Anything Gemma made up is thrown away, and Gemma is asked once more, told what was wrong.

3 · Decide

Your balance chain decides

Last balance + amount must equal the new balance, to the kobo. The account must be yours. The reference must be new. Only then: PAID.

Then · Learn

Gemma once, then instant

Your alerts come from the same one or two banks, in the same layout every time. After Gemma reads an alert and every value passes grounding, Alertproof remembers the words that sit before each value, like Avail Bal: NGN. The next alert in that layout is read in under a millisecond, through the same grounding and chain. It only keeps a learned format if re-reading the alerts it learned from gives exactly what Gemma read. Anything that doesn’t fit goes back to Gemma.

Why a fake can’t pass

A fake-alert app lets a fraudster type any sender, amount and wording. To pass Alertproof, it would also need your exact balance after your last alert, which only you and your bank know.

Tested, not promised

70 alerts. 10 formats. Real and fake.

We ran Gemma 3 4B on a laptop with no graphics card over SMS, push and email formats from GTBank, Access, Zenith, UBA, First Bank, OPay, Moniepoint and PalmPay. Every format got real credits, a debit, a fake with an invented balance, a fake with a round balance, an alert for someone else’s account, and an old alert sent again.

0 / 20invented-balance fakes released
10 / 10old alerts sent again, stopped
20 / 20genuine payments proven PAID
0.06 msper alert once a format is learned, 56 of 60 with no model

What we got wrong, and fixed

The first run released 4 fakes. All four were alerts for someone else’s account that also carried the trader’s exact balance, the worst case. Two were in formats that show only two account digits (Zenith’s 01**876**32, Access emails). Our grounding check demanded three, so the account check was skipped. We now accept two digits when they come straight after a mask, and only the digits the alert actually shows. Then we re-ran those formats: both fakes are now stopped, with no new false alarms.

The other two are a true limit. OPay and PalmPay notifications show no account at all. If such an alert also carries your exact balance to the kobo, nothing in it can tell it apart from a real one. In practice that means someone has already seen your latest alert, so keep your alerts private.

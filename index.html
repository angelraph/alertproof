For traders, POS agents and anyone paid by transfer

A fake alert can copy your bank’s words. It can’t know your balance.

The customer says “I don send am”, the phone buzzes, and you have five seconds to decide whether to hand over the goods. Alertproof reads the alert with Gemma on your own device, then checks it against the only thing a fraudster never has: the running balance in your real alerts.

How it works

  1. Your alerts become a chain

    Share or paste your own bank alerts, or type today’s balance from your bank app once. Every real alert’s balance follows from the one before it: last balance plus the amount.

  2. Gemma reads, code checks

    Every bank words alerts differently, and they keep changing. Gemma, an open model running on your laptop, reads any alert or receipt screenshot. Then plain code checks that every number Gemma read is really written in the alert. If it isn’t, the number is thrown away.

  3. Arithmetic decides

    PAID when the balance continues your chain exactly. NO RELEASE when it doesn’t, when it’s for another account, or when it’s an old alert sent again. CAN’T TELL when there isn’t enough to prove it, and it tells you what to check. The model never decides whether money is real.

  4. Screenshots are not money

    When a customer shows a “Transfer Successful” screenshot, Alertproof checks it was sent to your account, then tells you exactly what your next real alert must say: “CR ₦15,000 · Avail Bal ₦92,160.50”. When that alert lands, it turns green on its own.

It says it out loud

Your hands are busy at the counter. The answer is spoken in English or Pidgin: “No release am. This alert no follow your balance.”

Today’s money, proven

Every checked payment goes into a day book: what came in by transfer, from whom, which fakes were stopped, and whether your chain is unbroken.

Works without data

SMS alerts arrive when the internet doesn’t. On a laptop with Gemma, Alertproof checks alerts fully offline.

Why it has to be an open model

  • Your balance is the secret. The whole check works because nobody else knows your balance. Sending every alert to someone else’s cloud would hand over the one thing that protects you. With Gemma on your own laptop, it never leaves.
  • Free to run, so cheap to sell. A check costs nothing on your own machine, so a shop can pay a small monthly fee instead of a fee per alert.
  • Banks change their formats. With open weights we can test every new format ourselves, swap models, or fine-tune one on Nigerian alerts. A closed API can change under you.

What it can’t do

  • It needs a starting point: your balance from the bank app, or your own recent alerts. Until then every answer is CAN’T TELL, never PAID.
  • If you miss an alert (a charge, a debit), the chain breaks. Alertproof says so and asks you to set your balance again; it doesn’t guess.
  • Someone who has seen your latest alert knows your balance. Keep your alerts private.
  • Gemma on a laptop with no graphics card takes about half a minute per alert. Online Gemma takes seconds, but the alert text goes to Google’s hosted Gemma (nothing is stored).
